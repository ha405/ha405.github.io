I study how modern neural networks fail under distribution shift, federated training, and safety-critical settings. My work uses mechanistic interpretability to understand these failures and build more robust, efficient, and trustworthy learning systems.

I am a Research Assistant at the Centre for Urban Informatics, Technology and Policy (CITY) at LUMS, and a Graduate Teaching Fellow for AI 630: Responsible AI Engineering. I also work as a Founding Product Engineer at NEXA, building scalable distributed ML systems.

Research Focus

Primary Mechanistic Interpretability

Reverse-engineering internal circuits and failure modes in neural networks.

Primary AI Safety

Building reliable and interpretable systems that behave robustly under shift.

Primary Federated Learning

Analyzing representational misalignment and circuit collapse under Non-IID data.

Secondary Model Compression

Pruning and quantization methods that preserve useful representations.

Secondary Domain Generalization

Separating robust, domain-invariant circuits from shortcut features.
