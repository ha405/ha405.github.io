Research

I study what neural networks learn internally and why they behave in unsafe or unreliable ways, using mechanistic interpretability as the primary tool. Current work is on AI safety; earlier work covered federated learning, model compression, and domain generalization.

Research Areas

  • AI Safety current Emergent misalignment: how narrow fine-tuning yields broadly misaligned behaviour, and which circuits and features drive it.
  • Mechanistic Interpretability current Circuit extraction, sparse autoencoders, and probing to explain behaviour and failure at the level of model internals.
  • Federated Learning completed Representational misalignment in FedAvg under non-IID data.
  • Model Compression completed Pruning and quantization that preserve representations at extreme sparsity and low bit-widths.
  • Domain Generalization completed Separating domain-invariant circuits from shortcut features.

Publications

Mechanistic Evidence for Preserved-but-Misaligned Representations in Non-IID FedAvg
M. Haseeb, S. Masood, M. A. Sohail, M. F. Shoaib, M. Tahir · ICML 2026 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop

We ask why FedAvg degrades under non-IID data. Analysing sparse models with circuit extraction, Universal Sparse Autoencoders, and linear probes, we find that informative client features remain present in the global model even as accuracy falls. The failure is a misalignment between preserved representations and the classification head, not a loss of the representations themselves. This reframes "weight divergence" as an observable structural failure.

BaCP: Backbone Contrastive Pruning for Preserving Representations in Extremely Sparse Neural Networks
M. H. Khawaja, M. Haseeb, M. F. Shoaib, M. Tahir · Preprint

Highly sparse networks suffer representational collapse. BaCP adds a multi-objective contrastive loss that aligns sparse embeddings against pretrained, fine-tuned, and historical references. It preserves accuracy at up to 99% sparsity across CNNs, Vision Transformers, and language models.

Projects

Current — AI Safety

Emergent Misalignment Through Circuits
CITY, LUMS · with Dr. Muhammad Tahir · 2026 –

Narrow fine-tuning can produce broadly misaligned behaviour far outside the training distribution. I am using circuit-level analysis to locate the features and pathways that carry this behaviour, test whether they are shared across misalignment triggers, and evaluate interventions on model internals as a route to controlling it.

Completed — Federated Learning & Interpretability

Circuit Preservation in Federated Learning
CITY, LUMS · with Dr. Muhammad Tahir · 2025 – 2026

Led the project behind the ICML 2026 MI Workshop paper above: built an FL simulation over sparse, interpretable models and showed that non-IID aggregation misaligns rather than erases client circuits.

Completed — Domain Generalization

Interpretability for Domain Generalization
CITY, LUMS · with Dr. Muhammad Tahir

Used cross-domain data as a contrastive probe to isolate circuits stable across domains and separate them from shortcut pathways that fail out of distribution. Earlier, optimised learnable visual queries in ViTs with Group Relative Query Optimization, improving PACS generalization by 3%.

Completed — Model Compression

Quantization of Diffusion Transformers
CITY, LUMS & 10x Engineers · with Dr. Zubair Khalid

Developed a predictor conditioned on per-timestep log-SNR to set activation quantization parameters, cutting perceptual error (LPIPS) by 55%. Implemented Hessian-based GPTQ and Qronos for 4-bit weight quantization.

KL-Aware Quantization (KLAWQ)
CVGL, LUMS · with Dr. Murtaza Taj

Extended GPTQ with second-order curvature from KL-divergence and cross-entropy objectives, integrating distillation into post-training quantization. Reduced perplexity by 30% over GPTQ at equal bit-width.

Completed — Computer Vision

Single-Image Camera Calibration (SOFI-UGCL)
CVGL, LUMS · with Dr. Murtaza Taj

Combined a multi-scale deformable transformer (SOFI) predicting zenith and horizon with geometric constraints in the loss to recover intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters from one image.
