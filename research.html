Research

I study what neural networks learn internally and why they behave in unsafe or unreliable ways. Current work is on AI safety and emergent misalignment; earlier work covered federated learning, model compression, and domain generalization.

  • AI Safety current — Emergent misalignment from narrow fine-tuning; locating and controlling the circuits that carry it.
  • Mechanistic Interpretability current — Circuit extraction, sparse autoencoders, and probing.
  • Federated Learning completed — Representational misalignment in FedAvg under non-IID data.
  • Model Compression completed — Pruning and quantization preserving representations at extreme sparsity.
  • Domain Generalization completed — Domain-invariant vs. shortcut circuits.

Publications

  1. Mechanistic Evidence for Preserved-but-Misaligned Representations in Non-IID FedAvg
    M. Haseeb, S. Masood, M. A. Sohail, M. F. Shoaib, M. Tahir
    ICML 2026 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop

    Circuit extraction, USAEs, and linear probes show non-IID aggregation keeps client features intact but misaligns them with the classification head.

  2. BaCP: Backbone Contrastive Pruning for Preserving Representations in Extremely Sparse Neural Networks
    M. H. Khawaja, M. Haseeb, M. F. Shoaib, M. Tahir
    Preprint

    Multi-objective contrastive loss aligning sparse embeddings against pretrained, fine-tuned, and historical references; preserves accuracy at up to 99% sparsity.
